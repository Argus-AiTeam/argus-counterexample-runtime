# Infinite-quotient Andrews--Curtis obstruction boundary

## Scope verdict

No infinite-quotient separator is known for the GST \(P_3\) pair. Five broad
classes are now excluded by exact AC equivalences rather than by bounded
search:

1. every solvable quotient of \(F_2\), finite or infinite; and
2. every quotient factoring through
   \[
   B_3=\langle x,y\mid xyx=yxy\rangle
      =F_2/\langle\!\langle u\rangle\!\rangle .
   \]; and
3. more generally, every quotient in which the normal closure of the image
   of \(u\) is abelian; and
4. every quotient in which that normal closure is metabelian; and
5. every quotient killing \(v=x^3y^{-4}\), because the universal such
   quotient
   \[
   G_{3,4}=\langle x,y\mid x^3=y^4\rangle
   \]
   already contains an exact 13-move equivalence
   \((u,1)\sim_{\mathrm{AC}}(x,y)\).

The second class includes a finitely presented, infinite, non-solvable group
with a decidable exact normal form. Thus moving from finite groups to the most
natural one-relator non-solvable quotient does not produce the required
obstruction. The third class follows from an exact 68-move equivalence in
\[
G_{\mathrm{rel}}
=F_2/[\langle\!\langle u\rangle\!\rangle,
      \langle\!\langle u\rangle\!\rangle],
\]
not from a finite-dimensional approximation of its relation module.
The fourth follows by correcting the complete unprojected fiber of that
68-move endpoint in
\[
G_{\mathrm{met}}=F_2/R'',\qquad R=\langle\!\langle u\rangle\!\rangle.
\]
The fifth is independent of the preceding derived-length exclusions:
\(G_{3,4}\) is infinite and non-solvable, \(u\) normally generates the
whole group, and the equivalence is checked in its exact amalgam normal
form.

This does not prove AC equivalence in \(F_2\), handleslide triviality of
\(L_3\), or impossibility of a separator in an infinite quotient where the
normal closure of \(u\) is not metabelian.

## All solvable quotients collapse

Myasnikov's property (T) is transitivity of the extended Nielsen, equivalently
AC, transformations on normally generating \(n\)-tuples. His theorem and
Corollary 1 imply that every finitely generated relatively free solvable group
has property (T).

If \(Q\) is a solvable quotient of \(F_2\) of derived length at most \(d\),
then \(F_2\twoheadrightarrow Q\) factors through
\[
S_{2,d}=F_2/F_2^{(d)}.
\]
The images of \((u,v)\) and \((x,y)\) normally generate \(S_{2,d}\), so
property (T) gives an AC equivalence there. The quotient map sends the same
sequence to \(Q\). Therefore no solvable quotient can separate the pairs.

In the free metabelian quotient \(M_2=F_2/F_2''\), exact Magnus/Fox normal
forms give
\[
\operatorname{ab}(u)=(1,-1),\qquad
\operatorname{ab}(v)=(3,-4),\qquad
W\!\left(u(xy^{-1})^{-1}\right)=-X.
\]
The nonzero winding term is not invariant under the full AC action on this
normally generating pair: Myasnikov's theorem supplies an actual AC
equivalence in \(M_2\). Winding and relation-module invariants that separate
tuples normally generating a proper normal subgroup do not cross this
whole-group boundary.

## The braid-relator quotient also collapses

In \(B_3\), \(u=1\). Put
\[
c=yx,\qquad \Delta=xyx.
\]
The braid relation gives
\[
c y c^{-1}=x,\qquad c x c^{-1}=yxy^{-1},
\qquad \Delta x\Delta^{-1}=y.
\]
For \(v=x^3y^{-4}\), direct substitution yields
\[
x^{-1}=v\,(yv^{-1}y^{-1})\,(cvc^{-1}). \tag{1}
\]

Equation (1) lets AC moves accumulate three conjugates of the second
coordinate in the first, taking \((1,v)\) to \((x,v)\). Four conjugated copies
of \(x\) and three inverse copies then use
\[
v y^4x^{-3}=1
\]
to reach \((x,1)\). Independently,
\[
(x,y)\sim_{AC}(x,x)\sim_{AC}(x,1)
\]
because \(y=\Delta x\Delta^{-1}\). Hence
\[
(\bar u,\bar v)\sim_{AC}(\bar x,\bar y)\quad\text{in }B_3.
\]

The exact replay first uses the amalgamated-product normal form
\[
B_3\cong\langle a,b\mid a^2=b^3\rangle,\qquad a=xyx,\quad b=xy.
\]
For \(z=a^2=b^3\), every element has a unique reduced expression
\(z^k s_1\cdots s_m\), with alternating syllables from \(\{a\}\) and
\(\{b,b^2\}\). This gives a direct exact word-problem algorithm. Every step is

## The torus-relator quotient also collapses

Set
\[
G_{3,4}=F_2/\langle\!\langle v\rangle\!\rangle
=\langle x,y\mid x^3=y^4\rangle .
\]
This is an infinite non-solvable group with central element
\(z=x^3=y^4\) and noncyclic quotient \(C_3*C_4\).

For \(c=yx\), free reduction gives
\[
cyc^{-1}=u^{-1}x,\qquad cxc^{-1}=yxy^{-1}.
\]
Conjugating \(y^4=x^3\) by \(c\), and using centrality of \(z\), yields
\[
(u^{-1}x)^4=x^3.
\]
After inversion and rearrangement this is the exact normal-generation
identity
\[
x=(x^3ux^{-3})(x^2ux^{-2})(xux^{-1})u
  =u(x^2ux^{-2})(xux^{-1})u. \tag{2}
\]
The free-word identity
\[
uy=(xy)x(xy)^{-1}
\]
then gives
\[
y=u^{-1}(xy)x(xy)^{-1}. \tag{3}
\]
Thus \(G_{3,4}/\langle\!\langle u\rangle\!\rangle=1\), even though the
single element \(u\) cannot abstractly generate the noncyclic group.
Myropolska's Proposition 2.1 therefore shows
\(G_{3,4}\notin\mathfrak C\); her class-\(\mathfrak C\) theorem does not
decide this case.

Equations (2)--(3) compile to thirteen elementary AC moves:
\[
\begin{aligned}
(u,1)&\to(u,u)
\to(x^2ux^{-2},u)
\to(x^2ux^{-2},ux^2ux^{-2})\\
&\to(u,ux^2ux^{-2})
\to(xux^{-1},ux^2ux^{-2})\\
&\to(xux^{-1},ux^2ux^{-2}xux^{-1})
\to(u,ux^2ux^{-2}xux^{-1})\\
&\to(u,x)\to(u^{-1},x)\to(u^{-1},uy)
\to(y,uy)\to(y,x)\to(x,y).
\end{aligned}
\]
Every arrow is one coordinate conjugation, inversion, right
multiplication, or permutation. The primary replay decides every state in
the unique form \(z^ks_1\cdots s_m\), with alternating
\(s_i\in\{x,x^2\}\) and \(s_i\in\{y,y^2,y^3\}\), and expands the reverse to
23 elementary moves. An independent implementation uses the reduced
\(C_3*C_4\) word together with the weighted exponent
\(x\mapsto4,\ y\mapsto3\); this pair is faithful because it detects every
power of \(z\). Both replays are in
`research/torus_knot_ac_quotient/`.

Silver--Whitten--Williams prove that torus-knot groups contain infinitely
many pseudo-meridians, but their displayed killers are a different family
\(\mu_n=xa^{-n}\) after a generator change. They do not identify the
present \(u\), and pseudo-meridian, nonconjugacy, and non-automorphicity are
not invariants of a full AC component. The explicit equivalence above
decides this component without using that theorem.

not to the displayed images of \((x,y)\).  The images of \(x,y\) generate
all of \(S_4\), the normal closure of the image of \(u\) is all of \(S_4\),
and the two right residuals are the nonidentity double transpositions
\([1,0,3,2]\) and \([2,3,0,1]\).  Since equality in \(F_2\) would survive
every homomorphism, this exactly refutes the proposed free-group endpoint
for this correction.

The local cause is already visible in the inverse-root convention.  In

This does not separate Andrews--Curtis orbits.  It retires only the direct
lift of the reviewed quotient correction: a different free-group AC word
may still exist, while the finitary Andrews--Curtis theorem already rules
out using the \(S_4\) pair itself as a global invariant.

## Source-conditioned dynamic inverses still do not lift the correction

The first mechanical inverse overlay froze a child's forward trace at the
inverse node's own entry and then reversed that trace.  This is not the
inverse of the child's dynamic source-to-endpoint map.  Actual free-group
composition reproduces the Reviewer controls: the two orders leave first
residual lengths \(46/40\) for `b_positive`, \(66/56\) for `b_negative`,
and \(58/56\) for node 10/node 11.  The earlier claimed local identities
and their resulting \(S_4\) endpoint therefore do not bear on a genuine
dynamic inverse.

The corrected object is a source/target-certified arrow.  A terminal
template carries its actual source, target, and frozen elementary
conjugators; an inverse occurrence may reverse it only when the certified
target is exactly the current entry.  Direct free reduction in
\(F(a,b,s,t)\) gives zero residual in both composition orders for all four
templates and for the 12-move node-10 arrow with its 16-move node-11
reverse.  Identity, concat, repeat, and inverse preserve this law by
matching arrow boundaries and reversing occurrence order.

There is no universal ordinary pair-map substitute.  Across all 13,824
choices of second coordinate and bases in \(S_4\), each dynamic
\(b\)-template has image size 12 in 6,912 contexts, 15 in 4,608 contexts,
and 24 in only 2,304 contexts.  Explicit collisions therefore rule out a
total two-sided endpoint-map inverse, while leaving individual certified
arrows available.

An independent C++ replay consequently interprets every terminal node as a
relation of certified arrows: concat is relational composition, repeat is
finite relational power, and inverse is the converse relation.  This
includes every possible source-conditioned certificate branch and imports
no correction generator.  After all 602,083 nodes, 16,852 executed roots,
and 8,426 stage-2 records, the reachable relation remains a singleton with
stage endpoints
\[
\begin{aligned}
([1,3,0,2],[3,1,0,2]),\quad
([3,1,2,0],[2,0,1,3]),\quad
([3,2,0,1],[2,0,1,3]).
\end{aligned}
\]
The final target is still
\(([1,0,2,3],[0,2,3,1])\); both right residuals are the nonidentity
permutation \([2,3,0,1]\).  Since even the full certified-arrow relation
excludes the target, no stricter pairing of these terminal certificates can
repair this explicit lift in \(F_2\).

The compressed free reduction gate therefore remains closed.  This retires
only the dynamic Schreier--Peiffer lift, not general AC equivalence, and it
supplies no handle-slide obstruction.  Exact records and replay code are in
`research/free_group_ac_dynamic_inverse/`; the invalid frozen-trace control
is retained read-only in `research/free_group_ac_true_inverse/`.

## Corrected AK(3) stabilization boundary

The public Lisitsa/Zenodo certificate proves a strict rank-two equivalence
from the displayed length-25 presentation \(P\) to \(AK(3)\), not a stable
trivialization.  An independent free-group replay in
`research/ak3_stabilization_kernel/` verifies all 252 uncompressed S1 moves
and all 160 compressed S2 moves.  The exact endpoint is
\[
(a^3b^{-4},aba b^{-1}a^{-1}b^{-1})=(v,u),
\]
not \((a,b)\), and neither ledger contains AC4.  The replay also repairs one
published compressed label: S2 step 81 omits the initial \(a\) in the
conjugator; the five constituent S1 conjugations give
`CONJ aBAbAXaBabA` and reproduce the recorded next state.

The missing stable implication cannot be imported from MMS02.  Its alleged
unknot Wirtinger presentation prints
\[
x_{13}=x_5x_{12}x_5^{-1}
\]
where the diagram gives \(x_{13}=x_4x_{12}x_4^{-1}\).
Shehper et al. check that the printed presentation is not Wirtinger:
deleting different relators can give \(B_3\) or \(\mathbb Z\).  Their
corrected conclusion is only that \(P\) is AC-equivalent to \(AK(3)\), not
that \(P\) is thereby stably AC-trivial.  Hence the cited work supplies no
valid AC4 word, auxiliary generator-relator, or rank-two
stabilization-kernel class to extract.  This closes that proof-extraction
route without proving or refuting either strict or stable AC triviality of
\(AK(3)\).

The 2026 status remains consistent with this boundary:
arXiv:`2606.21611v1` calls strict \(AK(3)\) beyond current reach, while
arXiv:`2607.23611v1`, Theorems 3--4, connects two MS(3) presentations to
the still-open \(AK(3)\) class but not to the standard basis.  Myropolska's
Theorem 1.3, arXiv:`1304.2668v2`, separately rules out a separator in any
group with all maximal subgroups normal unless one already appears in
abelianization; it does not restore the missing stabilization.

## The literal AK(3) and P complexes are not thickenable

Thickenability must be tested on literal semigroup attaching words; strict
AC equivalence does not transfer it.  With uppercase denoting inverse
traversal, the two separately tested inputs are

\[
AK(3)=(\mathtt{abaBAB},\mathtt{aaaBBBB})
\]

and

\[
P=(\mathtt{ABaBAbaBBabAb},\mathtt{BAbbABabaBBa}).
\]

The direction, boundary basepoint, and unreduced letters are fixed in
`research/ak3_thickenability/inputs.json`.  Their vertex-link corner graphs
have four vertices \(a^\pm,b^\pm\).  Every possible orientable thickening
is represented by a cyclic occurrence order at \(a^+\) and \(b^+\), with
the paired orders reversed at \(a^-\) and \(b^-\).

That necessity statement is now self-contained and does not use Neuwirth's
inaccessible article body.  An embedding into a 3-manifold with boundary
first embeds into its double.  A regular neighborhood of either complex
collapses to the complex; the \(AK(3)\) relations directly give the trivial
group, and the exact strict \(P\)-to-\(AK(3)\) replay gives the same for
\(P\).  Hence the regular neighborhood has vanishing orientation character,
even if the ambient manifold is nonorientable.  A small sphere about the
unique vertex cuts out the literal corner graph.  Along each cut-open
generator edge, oriented normal-disk transport preserves the sheet order,
while the two intersections with the vertex sphere have opposite signs;
therefore the occurrence-paired orders at \(g^+\) and \(g^-\) are reverses.
The corner graph is connected, so its thin neighborhood in \(S^2\) has genus
zero and
\[
F=E-V+2.
\]
Thus every possible PL embedding induces exactly one of the canonical
paired spherical rotations covered by the existing enumeration.

For \(AK(3)\), fixing one occurrence in each cycle leaves exactly
\(5!6!=86\,400\) rotation systems.  A spherical link requires 11 face
cycles; exact exhaustion gives only 1, 3, 5, or 7, with maximum 7.

For \(P\), a spherical link would have 23 faces and therefore at least 19
digons.  Its three \(b^+b^-\) edges contribute at most two, so at least 17
must lie in the four \(a^\pm b^\pm\) parallel classes.  Run counting reduces
all \(10!\) cyclic \(a\)-orders to 13,824 orders of capacity 18.  Each
induces 18 directed adjacency requirements on the cyclic \(b\)-order.
Keeping all or omitting one gives 262,656 branches; every branch prescribes
either two distinct successors or two distinct predecessors for one
occurrence.  This exhausts the full \(10!13!\) rotation space without
sampling.

An independently implemented read-only replay reconstructs the corner
involutions from the four raw words and reproduces every face count, run
bound, and conflict witness.  Thus neither fixed presentation complex
embeds in a 3-manifold.  This retires only the direct-thickenability shortcut
through Lackenby Theorem 1.3.  It proves neither strict AC non-equivalence nor
handle-slide nontriviality, and `C2669-GLOBAL-OBSTRUCTION` remains open.
The Neuwirth DOI is retained only as an inconclusive literature record, not
as a premise of this conclusion.

## Remaining quotient boundary

A future quotient separator must be infinite and non-solvable, keep both
\(u\) and \(v\) nontrivial, keep the normal closure of \(u\) at derived
length at least three, have a decidable exact word problem, and carry an
invariant preserved by inversion, multiplication, permutation, and
arbitrary componentwise conjugation.
Nielsen classes, \(T\)-systems, finite approximations, abelian relation
modules, stable AC invariants, and every quotient with metabelian normal
closure of \(u\) do not meet that contract.

GST Section 7 still supplies the relevant one-way implication:
\[
L_3\text{ unstabilized handleslide-trivial}
\Longrightarrow
(u,v)\sim_{AC}(x,y)\text{ in }F_2.
\]
Only an actual separation in a quotient would obstruct the left-hand side.
The four collapses above provide no global handle-slide obstruction.
Within \(G_{\mathrm{met}}\), the axis subgroup cannot remove the defect and
the Nielsen-conjugated loop destroys its projected ideal. The complete
Schreier--Peiffer correction now shows why those subgroup obstructions could
not extend: the full based zero orbit is all of \(M^2\). The entire
\(F_2/R''\) route is therefore retired by an actual AC equivalence, not by a
finite search miss. It gives no handle-slide equivalence; a counterexample
still requires a materially different global obstruction.
