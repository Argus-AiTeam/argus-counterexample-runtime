# Finite-quotient Andrews-Curtis no-go for GST \(P_3\)

## Scope verdict

No Andrews-Curtis graph of a finite quotient of \(F_2\) can separate the GST
\(P_3\) relator pair from the standard free basis. This is an exact theorem,
not a bounded-search result. It retires the unadorned finite-quotient
Andrews-Curtis route.

It does **not** prove that \(P_3\) is Andrews-Curtis trivial in the infinite
free group, that \(L_3\) handleslides to the unlink, or that an ordinary
nonabelian Nielsen graph is connected.

## Exact relators and source crosswalk

GST Section 7 gives, for \(k=1\),
\[
\langle x_G,y_G\mid y_Gx_Gy_G=x_Gy_Gx_G,\quad
x_G^{n+1}=y_G^n\rangle .
\]
Set \(x=y_G\) and \(y=x_G\). For \(n=3\), this becomes
\[
P_3=\langle x,y\mid xyx=yxy,\quad x^3=y^4\rangle .
\]
Choose the corresponding ordered relator pair
\[
u=xyx\,y^{-1}x^{-1}y^{-1},\qquad
v_3=x^3y^{-4}.
\]
Thus \(P_3=\langle x,y\mid u,v_3\rangle\). This is exactly the \(n=3\)
Akbulut--Kirby pair written in Borovik--Lubotzky--Myasnikov (BLM), not merely
a similar presentation. Reversing an equality would invert the corresponding
relator and would not change its AC class.

## Direct normal-generation proof

Let \(H=F_2/\langle\!\langle u,v_3\rangle\!\rangle\). In \(H\),
\[
xyx=yxy,\qquad x^3=y^4.
\]
For \(c=yx\), the braid relation gives
\[
cyc^{-1}=yxyx^{-1}y^{-1}=x,\qquad
cxc^{-1}=yxy^{-1}.
\]
Conjugating \(x^3=y^4\) by \(c\) therefore gives
\[
yx^3y^{-1}=x^4.
\]
Substituting \(x^3=y^4\) into the left side yields
\[
x^3=y^4=yx^3y^{-1}=x^4,
\]
so \(x=1\). The braid relation then reads \(y=y^2\), hence \(y=1\).
Therefore \(H=1\) and
\[
\langle\!\langle u,v_3\rangle\!\rangle=F_2.
\]
This supplies the normal-generation hypothesis rather than assuming it from
the phrase "presentation of the trivial group."

## Abelianization and explicit Nielsen path

The exponent-sum rows of \(u\) and \(v_3\), in the ordered basis \((x,y)\),
are
\[
M=
\begin{pmatrix}
1&-1\\
3&-4
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det M=-1.
\]
Hence \(M\in\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Z)\). More explicitly, for commuting
elements \(a,b\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
(ab^{-1},a^3b^{-4})
&\sim_N(ab^{-1},a^2b^{-3})\\
&\sim_N(ab^{-1},ab^{-2})\\
&\sim_N(ab^{-1},b^{-1})\\
&\sim_N(a,b^{-1})
\sim_N(a,b).
\end{aligned}
\]
The first three moves replace the second entry by the second times the inverse
of the first; the fourth multiplies the first entry by the inverse of the
second; the last inverts the second entry. These are elementary Nielsen moves.

## Application of BLM Theorem 1.1

BLM define \(d_G(G)\) to be the least number of normal generators of \(G\)
and \(N_k(G)\) to be the normally generating \(k\)-tuples. Their Theorem 1.1
states:

> If \(G\) is finite and \(k\geq\max\{d_G(G),2\}\), two tuples in
> \(N_k(G)\) are AC-equivalent in \(G\) if and only if their images are
> AC-equivalent in \(G/[G,G]\).

Now let \(\phi:F_2\twoheadrightarrow G\) be any finite quotient and write
\(a=\phi(x)\), \(b=\phi(y)\).

1. Since \(G\) is generated by \(a,b\), \(d_G(G)\leq2\), so
   \(k=2\geq\max\{d_G(G),2\}\).
2. The pair \((a,b)\) generates, hence normally generates, \(G\).
3. Normal generation of \(F_2\) by \((u,v_3)\) implies normal generation of
   \(G\) by \((\phi(u),\phi(v_3))\).
4. In \(G/[G,G]\), the displayed Nielsen path carries
   \((\phi(u),\phi(v_3))\) to \((a,b)\).

All hypotheses of Theorem 1.1 therefore hold, and
\[
(\phi(u),\phi(v_3))\sim_{AC}(\phi(x),\phi(y))
\quad\text{in every finite quotient }G.
\]
BLM also state this application directly for their full Akbulut--Kirby family.
For campaign 2669, the direct relator crosswalk above shows that their
\(n=3\) pair is GST \(P_3\).

The same proof gives the useful general boundary: if an ordered pair of words
normally generates \(F_2\) and its exponent-sum matrix lies in
\(\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb Z)\), then no finite-quotient AC graph separates that
pair from the standard basis.

## Ordinary Nielsen boundary

In an abelian group, componentwise conjugation is trivial, so AC equivalence
reduces to Nielsen equivalence. The explicit path above therefore also rules
out every **abelian** Nielsen-class separation.

For a nonabelian finite quotient, BLM conclude AC equivalence, not ordinary
Nielsen equivalence. A nonabelian Nielsen graph could still have more
components. That fact alone would not be a handle-slide obstruction: GST show
that a handle slide changes the meridian-relator tuple by multiplying one
relator by a conjugate of another, which is an AC move and need not be an
ordinary Nielsen move. Accordingly, this campaign does not retire ordinary
nonabelian Nielsen classes as abstract objects; it excludes them as evidence
until an exact decorated object is specified and every allowed handle slide is
proved to preserve its proposed Nielsen class.

Any next quotient-like obstruction must retain data absent from the unadorned
AC tuple, such as component-labelled peripheral structure, selected meridians
and longitudes, cocore markings, or a boundary-parametrized relative
four-dimensional trace, and must prove invariance under all unstabilized
handle slides. Merely increasing the finite-group order cannot evade the
theorem.
