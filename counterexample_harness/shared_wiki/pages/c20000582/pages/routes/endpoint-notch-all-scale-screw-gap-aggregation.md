# Endpoint-notch all-scale screw-gap aggregation

The reviewed prefix theorem gives a normalized canonical screw gap
strictly above \(3/700\) at every admissible even depth.  The fixed
complete form, mod-\(16\) weight transfer, and central-phase thickening
are also available.  Passing that prefix gap to the canonical longest
final envelope still requires control of all later bands.

A fixed number of immediately coarser bands cannot provide such control
through an absolute raw-tail estimate.  For an old depth \(d=42\), let
\(J=D_{m,\epsilon}-d\) and choose the genuine controller
\[
 (a,b,n)=\left(0,2^{S_d+2J-1},n_0\right),
\]
with \(n_0\) selecting a real final parent wave.  Across the three nodes
of the old screw, every future band strictly below the final top has
integer phase shift and affine tent, hence zero second difference.  The
final top instead has phase shifts \(-1/2,-1\), giving exact
second-difference magnitude
\[
 \left(8-2^{6-J}\right)c_{S_m}>6c_{S_m}.
\]
This is far larger than the prefix gap and holds inside the genuine
finite carrier for both parity shifts.

The result rules out only a fixed-window **absolute** future-tail
majorant.  It does not determine the final min-plus envelope because
the active controller may switch between screw nodes.  A positive
fixed-\({\mathsf Q}_m\) floor would require a controller-sensitive or
averaged persistence theorem; the opposite branch still requires an
exact unbounded family of final-envelope averages tending to zero.

The actual \(3/700\) gap does support a conditional phase transfer:
on the central share \(2^{-80}\), the perturbation square is at most
\(5/2^{24}<(3/1400)^2\).  Hence a final-envelope representative gap
would persist above \(3/1400\) on exact incidence-weight share
\(1/(4896\cdot2^{80})\).  The missing premise is precisely the
final-envelope gap.  Unsigned curvature cannot supply it: the branch
triples \((0,1,0)\) and \((1,0,1)\) have opposite second differences,
but their pointwise minimum is constant.
