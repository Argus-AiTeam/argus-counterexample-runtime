# Endpoint-notch coherent signed Tao boundary

For the frozen endpoint-notch coefficient sequence,
\[
b_{m,n}=\sqrt{1-\frac1m}\left(
A^{-n/m}-R_m^{-1/(m(m+1))}A^{-n/(m+1)}\right),
\]
the proposed coherent subtraction asks one finite-dimensional oscillator
family to inherit Tao's pointwise orthogonality at every exponent
\(\varepsilon_m=1/m\).

That simultaneous requirement is impossible. For fixed scale \(n\), point
\(p\), and horizontal derivative \(W\), the cross-scale inner products are
bounded coefficients of a power series \(F\). Endpointwise orthogonality
forces \(F(A^{-1/m})=0\) for all large \(m\). A self-contained Jensen
zero-density estimate shows that, when \(A>e\), these zeros force
\(F\equiv0\). All horizontal derivatives at distinct scales would then be
pairwise orthogonal and nonzero, contradicting finite target dimension.

The exact proof is
`research/routes/p2_endpoint_scale_notch/signed_tao_incompatibility/coherence_boundary.md`.
It retires the common endpoint-orthogonal subtraction. It does not produce a
Euclidean embedding of the notch blocks, and it does not exclude
\(m\)-dependent oscillators or a materially different direct signed map.
Within the current preregistered frontier, the remaining obstruction
mechanism is a genuinely full-block PSD or exterior-power certificate.
